# EXP-011 — Polymarket daily "highest temperature" markets: is a public ensemble forecast better than the price?

Registered 2026-10-14 before any forecast-vs-outcome statistic was computed. Scope: a feasibility
study of the one prediction-market line that has real data; not a trading system. Disclosure: the
owner runs a separate "Weather Quant" project (VPS, live canary) that may already cover this; this
study does not read it. Three public weather bots were audited first (research/results/AUDIT-001.md):
none contains verifiable profit; one's backtest is circular; one misparses the Polymarket buckets.

## Data (all public, fetched 2026-10-14)
- Polymarket Gamma: 899 closed "Highest temperature in <city> on <date>?" events, 20 cities,
  2025-06-19 .. 2026-10-13, 11 buckets each, resolution text names the station (e.g. NOAA LaGuardia
  hourly "Temp" column, max of hourly readings).
- Polymarket CLOB `prices-history` per bucket token (hourly fidelity).
- Open-Meteo GFS ensemble (31 members, 0.25°), `past_days=92`, daily max at the resolution station's
  coordinates. Only the last ~92 days are available without the rate-limited previous-runs API, so
  Part B covers 2026-07-14 .. 2026-10-13.

## Decision time
06:00 UTC on the observation day D: the 00Z GFS run is public by ~05Z, and for cities in the
Americas and Europe the day's maximum has not happened yet. Asian cities are excluded from Part B
(their afternoon is under way at 06Z). Market price = last CLOB trade at or before 06:00 UTC D.

## Part A — market calibration (descriptive, all 899 events)
Reliability of bucket prices at 06:00 UTC D (and at 12:00 UTC D-1) against resolution: Brier score,
reliability table by price decile, and the pooled "sum of bucket prices" overround. No gate; this
tells us how much a forecast would have to beat.

## Part B — ensemble vs market (Americas + Europe, ~13 cities x ~90 days)
p_model(bucket) = share of 31 members whose rounded daily max falls in the bucket, after a
station-bias correction fitted on the FIRST 45 days only (per-city mean member error), then
smoothed with a Gaussian kernel of sigma = member spread (floor 1 °F). Evaluated on the LAST 45 days.
Statistics: Brier score of p_model vs market price (paired, per bucket); log-loss; and a simulated
rule: buy the YES of a bucket when p_model − price >= 0.10 and price <= 0.60, stake 1 unit, payoff
at resolution, Polymarket taker fee as reported by the market's fee field (0 if none), spread from
the book not modelled (one-tick worse than last trade assumed: +0.01).

## Gates (Part B, last 45 days only)
LEAD: p_model Brier < market Brier with a paired bootstrap 95 % CI excluding zero, AND the simulated
rule's mean return per unit > 0 with p < 0.05 on >= 40 bets. Anything less: "no evidence that a free
ensemble beats the market at 06Z" and the line is parked until a longer forecast archive is built
(the VPS can fetch previous-runs daily and accumulate a lead-time-stratified archive).

## What this cannot show
Whether a *paid* or better-calibrated forecast beats the market; whether fills at the last-trade
price are available (bucket liquidity is 2–25k USD, spread 0.001–0.02); latency effects at model
release times, which is where the practitioner guides locate the edge and which requires minute
data at 04–06Z.
